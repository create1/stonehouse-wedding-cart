01 — The wood

Why Madrone

Madrone, Arbutus menziesii, is a Pacific hardwood with a long reputation on the woodpile. This is what that means when you are buying a cord.

  1. 01Weight in the fire

    Madrone is dense. A piece feels heavy for its size, and that weight is what a long, steady fire is made of.

  2. 02Coals, not just flame

    It is known for holding a coal bed after the flames settle. That lingering heat is the part you actually sit with.

  3. 03A face you can name

    Smooth, reddish bark and a pale, tight grain on the split. In a mixed pile, madrone is the wood you can pick out by sight.

Like any hardwood, it is at its best after it has had time to dry. Ask how this wood was seasoned when you order.

02 — The measure

$700 for a full cord.

A cord is a stack that occupies 128 cubic feet. The usual picture is 4 feet high, 4 feet deep, and 8 feet long. The air between the pieces counts. It is not 128 cubic feet of solid wood.

Piece length varies. Sixteen-inch wood is common, and a full cord of those pieces is three rows, each 4 feet high and 8 feet long. One row alone is a face cord, sometimes called a rick. That is not a cord, and it is not this price.

The $700 is the price of the wood for one full cord. Delivery, and any tax, are confirmed with your request. They are not charged on this page.
